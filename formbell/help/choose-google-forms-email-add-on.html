How to choose an email add-on for Google Forms

You can compare these add-ons on four things: when they send, who they send to, how much you can send for free, and what you can check afterwards.

First, check what Google Forms already does

In Google Forms™ you can get an email when a new response arrives. If you collect email addresses, you can also send respondents a copy of their answers. Follow Google's instructions to change either setting.

For emails that depend on an answer, or a message in your own words, you can use an add-on.

Five questions to ask

  1. Does the email depend on an answer? For example, only refund requests go to billing. Check whether conditions are in the free plan or only in a paid one.
  2. Who gets it? One address per email, several addresses, CC and BCC, or an address the respondent typed in.
  3. How much can you send for free? Look for a daily or monthly email cap and a limit on how many notifications you can set up.
  4. Can you see what happened? When an email doesn't arrive, check the record to see what happened before you try a fix.
  5. What does the plan you need cost? Compare the yearly price of the plan that covers your answers to the first three questions.

Which one fits

If you want to email one person when an answer matches, and you want conditions on a free plan, you can use Formbell, Form Notify or Form Mailer. Formbell's free plan runs one rule at a time, so compare that with the other free limits for your form.

If one email should reach several people, or you need CC and BCC, pick an add-on that lists them. You can send each Formbell rule to one address.

If you need to check later whether an email went out, look for a send record. In Formbell you can see Sent, Failed or Unknown for recorded attempts. Sent means Google accepted the email, not that it reached the inbox.

If you only need respondents to get a copy of their answers, start with the Google Forms setting above.
